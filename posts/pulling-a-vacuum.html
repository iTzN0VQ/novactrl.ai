Pulling a vacuum — the right way

Most techs evacuate through the SMAN with Schrader cores still in. It works — barely. The pro method gets you a faster pull-down, accurate readings, and a real decay test.

Vacuum is the part of the install nobody respects until two years later when the compressor fails on warranty. The system you "evacuated to 500 microns" was probably sitting at 1,500+ because of what your SMAN was actually reading. Here's the procedure that gets you an honest number.

The procedure

  1. Remove the Schrader cores with a core removal tool — Appion MGAVCT or Yellow Jacket. The cores are the #1 flow restriction in your evacuation path.
  2. Hook a standalone micron gauge (Testo 552i, Fieldpiece SRG2, Appion AV-760) directly to one service port with a short rated hose. Skip the manifold.
  3. Pull vacuum from the other port straight to the pump with a 1/4" or 3/8" rated vacuum hose. Again, no SMAN in the loop.
  4. Decay test: hold below 500 microns for 10 minutes. Climbing → leak. Plateau → moisture. Holds → charge it.

Why this beats pulling through the SMAN

Your SMAN's built-in micron gauge reads the manifold's internal pressure — not the system's. That's the whole problem in one sentence.

On top of that:

  • Schrader cores choke flow and add 30–60 minutes to the pull-down.
  • Hoses and manifold fittings add leak points that ruin decay tests.
  • The contractor next to you thinks he pulled to 500 microns when the system is actually sitting at 1,500+.
Field rule. If your micron gauge is downstream of any restriction — Schrader cores, manifold body, hose fittings — it's not reading the system. It's reading the side of the restriction you're sitting on.
