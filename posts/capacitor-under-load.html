Capacitor under-load test

A lying meter sends you to the wrong part. Measure under load. Trust the numbers.

An ohmmeter on a weak capacitor will tell you the cap is fine. The capacitor only shows its real face when it's doing the job — passing current to start or run a motor. The dual-clamp method tests it there.

The formula

MFD = (Amps × 2652) ÷ Volts
Within ±6% of nameplate = healthy. Outside that range = replace it.

Procedure — dual clamp method

  1. System running. Capacitor needs to be under load — the motor it serves should be operating.
  2. Clamp meter on the motor lead coming off the capacitor (the C-side terminal on a HERM or FAN cap). Read Amps.
  3. Volt meter across the capacitor terminals — the two posts you just clamped between. Read Volts.
  4. Plug in: MFD = (Amps × 2652) ÷ Volts. Compare to the nameplate MFD rating.
  5. Verdict: ±6% = good. Anything outside that range — replace.

Example

Run capacitor rated 45 MFD ± 6%. Measured under load: 3.8 A at 240 V.

MFD = (3.8 × 2652) ÷ 240 = 41.99 MFD

Nameplate range: 42.3 – 47.7 MFD. Measured: 41.99. That's just under spec — weak. Replace it before the next heat wave does it for you.

Why this beats the cap function on your meter

  • The meter's built-in cap test measures at rest. A cap that's leaky only under voltage tests clean cold.
  • Heat changes MFD. The cap on the unit at 110°F ambient is not the cap on your bench.
  • Under-load testing tells you what the motor is actually seeing — which is the only number that matters.
